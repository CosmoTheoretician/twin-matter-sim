# jp-petit.org Batch Analysis

All pages are from **jp-petit.org**, the personal site of Jean-Pierre Petit, a French former CNRS plasma physicist and astrophysicist, comic-book author and activist. To save space, URLs are given as paths after `jp-petit.org/`. Several URLs appear twice in the batch, once with http and once with https. Each is listed only once here. Many summaries were based only on navigation text, so where a page's subject is inferred from its title or URL, I've said so.

## 3. Most Interesting and Noteworthy Pages

1. **A fake biography of a real mathematician:** `science/maths_f/obj_geom/withney.html`
   - The page claims Hassler Whitney was a circus tightrope walker who invented a two-ribbed umbrella and died falling into a crowd.
   - This is an invented legend about a major mathematician, presented alongside a genuine singularity (x² = y²z). It is a striking example of the site's mix of real mathematics and tongue-in-cheek fabrication.

2. **The skeptic who is also a believer:** `Divers/Paranormal/Paranormal6.htm`, `Divers/Faux/Faux_egyptien.htm`, `CHRONIQUES/mystery_missile/contrail.htm` versus `OVNIS/recuperation_sur_epave.htm`
   - On the skeptical side, Petit debunks a "paranormal" feat with a topology puzzle. He also confesses to planting a fake Egyptian fresco as a lesson in critical thinking, and admits he was fooled by the 2010 "mystery missile," along with Michio Kaku.
   - Yet he treats the alien-autopsy footage as possibly authentic, reading hand-shaped impressions as a six-fingered "piano-chord" control system.

3. **Convicted of defamation by a nuclear official:** `jugement/jugement00.htm`, `jugement/commentaires_jugement.htm`
   - Petit publishes his own appeal conviction (2003), won by a former director of the Marcoule nuclear centre.
   - He argues he lost on a procedural rule of the 1881 press law (deadline to submit evidence) rather than on the merits.

4. **A personal elegy in song:** `AVANT_DE_VOUS_QUITTER/Chansons/La_Poule_Noire.htm`
   - A song dedicated to his son Jean-Christophe, who died in 1990.
   - Its refrain "Lanturlu" echoes the name of the hero of his comic series. It is a moving contrast to the polemical tone of most of the site.

5. **Instructions for weapons on a public website:** `dangers/MICRO_ONDES/arme_micro_onde_domestique.htm` ("how to turn your microwave into a directed-energy weapon") and `Presse/ARMES/antimatiere_dans_cristaux.htm` (antiprotons caged in a gold lattice as a bomb)
   - Both are unusual and provocative subjects for a former research scientist to publish.

6. **Clubbers with implanted payment chips:** `BIG BROTHER/nightclubs_avec_puces.htm`
   - The Baja Beach Club in Barcelona really did offer VIP access and payment by VeriChip implant.
   - It is a real, early example of consumer RFID implants, and the core of the site's surveillance dossier.

7. **Turning a cube inside out:** `maths_f/obj_geom/mod_cent_ret_c_vj.html`, `deb_ret_cube.htm`
   - Petit designed a polyhedral version of Bernard Morin's sphere eversion in 1991, using a cardboard cube, which was later turned into a virtual reality object.
   - This is a genuine, original contribution to mathematical visualization.

8. **Fringe theory with a peer-reviewed past:** `science/f300/f3200/f3201.htm`
   - The twin-universe paper was published in *Astrophysics and Space Science* (1995), so the site's alternative cosmology has at least one foot in the academic literature.
   - The 2025 "OMERTA" page shows the dispute with the establishment is still ongoing.

9. **Covert nuclear tests in a coal mine?** `Presse/Moronvilliers.htm`, `Gardanne/rapport*.htm`
   - Petit claims France may have carried out covert underground nuclear tests in the Gardanne coal mine.
   - He backs this with transcripts of the mine's health-and-safety committee meetings.

10. **Small curiosities**
    - **Souriau's word game:** `humour/humour_contrepet.htm` gives the solution to a contrepèterie invented by the eminent mathematician Jean-Marie Souriau. The answer is "particule élémentaire".
    - **Cynicism about string theory:** `science/gal_port/misho_kaku.htm` recounts an early superstring conference opening speech that measured the field's success by paper count, not predictions.
    - **A priority claim from a comic:** `Presse/aspirisouffle.htm` claims priority for a suction-nozzle design first drawn in Petit's 1979 comic.
    - **A slow-selling book and a helicopter maker:** `science_culture_pour_tous/science_culture_pour_tous.htm` says the book sold about 1,000 copies in five years. It also notes that Eurocopter reprinted his helicopter comic *La Passion Verticale*.
    - **A future date:** `bd_lanturlu/bd_lanturlu.html` carries the date 21 July 2026, probably a dynamic date script or an error.
