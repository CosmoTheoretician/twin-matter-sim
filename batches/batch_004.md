# Analysis of jp-petit.org Page Summaries

All pages come from **jp-petit.org**, the personal website of Jean-Pierre Petit. He is a French former CNRS researcher (plasma physics/MHD, later cosmology), a science popularizer and comics author, and a ufologist. The site is mostly in French and mixes his own physics work, mathematics outreach, political commentary, UFO material, and personal content.

*Data-quality note:* Many summaries describe pages whose extracted text was only navigation, notably the `jugement0X` series, `f113`, `f114`, and the Kush pages. Several summaries are also cut off mid-sentence. Classifications for those pages rely on URL and title, not content.

## 3. Most Interesting and Noteworthy Pages

1. **Sphere eversion with Bernard Morin** (science/maths_f/Retournement_sphere/Retournement_sphere4.htm). Morin, a *blind* mathematician, devised a famous sphere-eversion construction, and Petit illustrated it in a 1979 *Pour la Science* article. This is a genuine, respected contribution to mathematical visualization.

2. **Boy surface story and 1981 CRAS note** (obj_geom/boy.html, maths_f/boy/f5101.htm). The pages tell an anecdote about Werner Boy, Hilbert's student, and reproduce a peer-reviewed Petit–Souriau parametrization of the surface. Together with the Italian Cross-cap → Boy tutorials by Andrea Sambusetti, they show the site's strongest mainstream content.

3. **The "monohedron"** (obj_geom/mono_transp.htm). Petit presents a self-invented "polyhedron" with one face, one edge, and no vertices. It is a curious claim that bends standard definitions.

4. **Public fight with CNRS evaluators** (blanchard.htm) and **defamation conviction** (jugement/commentaires_jugement.htm). These pages document Petit's open letter to rapporteur Alain Blanchard and his account of losing the right to present evidence under Article 55 of the 1881 press law. They show the author's long conflict with institutional science.

5. **Sakharov thread** (Fin_discours_Sakharov_1975.html, papers/cosmo/Sakharov/, 2006 seminar proposal). Petit roots the Janus model in Andrei Sakharov's twin-universe idea. The site honours Sakharov as a physicist and as a dissident, and the seminar proposal to the IAP reportedly went unanswered.

6. **A ufologist attacking ufologists** (Von_Ludwiger.htm, camelot_project.htm). Petit criticizes credentialed "ufologue-savants" for producing no peer-reviewed work, and calls Project Camelot/Avalon (David Icke's reptilians) disinformation. Meanwhile the same site promotes the UMMO case (Guide_eng.htm) and UFO-science fundraising. The **Paranormal debunking series** (Paranormal2/5) adds to this mix of skepticism and belief.

7. **Benveniste's appeal to homeopaths** (Benveniste_aux_homeopathes.htm). The page preserves a historical document from the "water memory" affair: a 2003 fundraising letter to about 15,000 homeopaths.

8. **"Astrophysics @ home" / Epistémotron** (Frederic_Baudemont1.htm, Didacticiel.htm). This is an early-2000s citizen-science effort with open-source N-body simulations of "twin matter," pitched so that "even a 10-year-old" could follow.

9. **Unexpected Egyptology papers** (papers/egyptology/). An astrophysicist's site hosts papers on Egyptian cubit rods and stone-sawing techniques.

10. **Putin astrology profile on a "scientific website"** (Divers/profils_astrologiques/Poutine.htm). It contrasts sharply with the site's self-description.

11. **Ambiguous satire on Scout hand signs** (BIG BROTHER/signe_main_bush.htm). The page is half joke, half conspiracy theory, and its intent is genuinely unclear.

12. **"La ballade des chercheurs"** (Chansons/Science.htm) and the song collection. Petit records his own songs, including a satirical ballad about scientists. The "Jeux interdits" page includes a candid apology for wrong notes.

13. **Contact guidelines** (Contact/Contact.htm). Petit reports receiving 150–200 emails a day and spending 4–5 hours daily processing them. He bans jokes and drawings, and he treats reader submissions as a kind of crowdsourced journal.

14. **Web-history relics** (virt_real.htm, instruc_dowload.htm). The pages give instructions for the long-dead Cosmo Player VRML plugin, with a "20-minute" dial-up download estimate. At the other end of the timeline, **bonnettereseau.htm** offers 3D-printable files for a smartphone spectroscope.

15. **Mainstream voice on an alternative site** (gal_port/vigier.htm). The obituary of de Broglie's student Jean-Pierre Vigier was written by Académie des Sciences member Jean-Claude Pecker.
