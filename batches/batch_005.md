## 3. Most interesting and noteworthy pages

1. **Mathematical in-jokes posing as real entries**
   - `/science/maths_f/obj_geom/withney.html` claims Whitney was a circus tightrope walker who invented the "Whitney umbrella" for his act and died falling into the crowd. This is fabricated: Hassler Whitney was a mathematician (and mountaineer).
   - `/science/maths_f/obj_geom/lactoedre.html` describes a "lactoèdre" ("milk-hedron") supposedly conjectured by Hilbert. It credits "Grossman 1956" and researchers "Iéronimus Holstein and Miroslav Milka", and calls the surface's equation an open problem. The names and the milk-carton pun strongly suggest a hoax.
   - Both sit alongside serious topology pages, so readers could easily be misled.

2. **Genuine research-level mathematics outreach**
   - `/maths_f/maths_f.htm` cites a 1981 *Comptes Rendus* paper by Petit and Souriau giving an analytic parametrization of Boy's surface.
   - The sphere-eversion series (e.g. `/maths_f/Retournement_sphere/Retournement_sphere3.htm`) explains Bernard Morin's "elementary catastrophes" (elbow, mandarin slice, trousers).
   - `/maths_f/obj_geom/boycube2.html` shows Anthony Phillips, a known topologist, engaging with Petit's polyhedral models.
   - This is the most credible material on the site and contrasts sharply with the fringe sections.

3. **CNRS assessing Petit alongside Benveniste** (`/science/nouveau/courrier_cnrs_mars_2005.htm`). During the 2005 World Year of Physics, *Le Courrier du CNRS* dossiers placed Petit next to Jacques Benveniste of "water memory" fame. The page announces this without saying what the CNRS concluded.

4. **The rejected 1992–94 galaxy simulations** (`/Cosmo_astro/animation_1992.htm`, `/science/f300/f3900/f3901.htm`). Petit says about a dozen journals refused his "ghost matter" spiral-galaxy simulations. He names an *A&A* editor and appeals to others to revive the work. This offers a window into his long-running conflict with peer review.

5. **AI-generated literature in 2025** (`/AVANT_DE_VOUS_QUITTER/…/nouvelles_crees_par_les_IA-et_les_internautes.html`). Fables and stories written with ChatGPT and Claude (Nov–Dec 2025) appear on a site whose oldest pages carry hit counters from 2000. It shows how long the site has been maintained.

6. **Sakharov as a recurring figure.** There is a `Sakharov/` folder in `/papers/cosmo/` and a Sakharov speech page filed under Israel/Palestine (`/Israel_Palestine/Fin_discours_Sakharov_1975.html`). Sakharov proposed twin-universe ideas in 1967, which Petit cites as a precursor. Filing his Nobel lecture under a political folder is an odd editorial choice.

7. **A skeptic who also endorses UFO claims**
   - `/Divers/Paranormal/Paranormal4.htm` debunks "magical" speed with physics and reaction-time mechanics.
   - The same site promotes UFO accounts (`/NOTES_DE_LECTURE/Faded_giant/…`, the 1967 Malmstrom missile shutdown claim), MHD readings of the B-2 (`/nouv_f/B2/B2_7.htm`) and the Pantone "water engine" (`/Presse/TF1_Pantone.htm`).
   - The contrast captures the site's mixed character.

8. **Area 51 satellite-image forensics** (`/VIDEOS_plongees_Google_Earth/Gros_plans/Aire51_etat_anterieur.htm`). Petit compares 1958 and 1968 imagery to argue a sunken runway section was unusable. It is an early (2005) example of amateur Google Earth investigation.

9. **Primary documents from the Gardanne mine closure** (`/Gardanne/rapport11.htm` and related pages). These are transcripts of workplace-safety committee meetings in which unions and management argue over unreported underground damage. Such labor-history material is rarely published.

10. **The Béryl nuclear-test accident** (`/Divers/Nucleaire_souterrain/in_ecker.htm`). This covers the real 1962 In Ekker accident, a historically significant and under-publicized radiation exposure event.

11. **Open server archives** (`/papers/CRAS/`, `/papers/MHD/`, `/papers/egyptology/`). Directory listings expose Petit's whole publication record: CRAS papers from 1969–1981, shock-tube work presented in Warsaw, Moscow and Beijing, and an unexpected Egyptology folder. They also reveal an old Apache 2.4.7 server.

12. **Satire of academia** (`/Divers/CNRS/autant_de_cons.htm`, the "Wurmstein-Paparazzi test", and `/science/gal_port/Lecomte.htm`). The latter invents a fake professor of "permutational linguistics" whose inventions include the bottle-opener sock. They reflect Petit's comic-book sensibility and his grudges with institutions.
