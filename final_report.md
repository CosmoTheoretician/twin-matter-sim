## 1. General Summary

jp-petit.org is the large, long-running personal website of **Jean-Pierre Petit**. He is a French former CNRS researcher in plasma physics and magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) who later worked in cosmology. He is also a science popularizer, author of the *Lanturlu* comic series, a painter and songwriter, and a ufologist. The site is mostly in French, with some English and Italian pages. Some pages carry hit counters from around 2000, and the site was still being updated in late 2025.

The site serves several purposes at once.

- **Promoting Petit's own physics.** Its centre is the **Janus / twin-universe cosmology**. This model posits negative-mass "ghost" matter that repels ordinary matter, offered as an alternative to dark matter, dark energy and black holes. Petit presents it as continuing Andrei Sakharov's ideas.
  - Some of this work appeared in peer-reviewed journals, including *Modern Physics Letters A* (1988), *Il Nuovo Cimento B* (1994) and *Astrophysics and Space Science* (1995).
  - It remains outside mainstream cosmology.
- **Teaching science.** The site offers:
  - courses on general relativity and on group theory in J.-M. Souriau's symplectic style;
  - a substantial, genuinely respected collection on **topology**: sphere eversion, Boy's surface, the cross-cap and paper models.
- **Documenting a feud with institutional science.** Pages cover peer-review disputes, CNRS evaluations, attacks on journal editors and a defamation conviction.
- **Activism and commentary.** Topics include:
  - surveillance ("Big Brother", RFID implants);
  - weapons and the nuclear sector;
  - Israel/Palestine;
  - US foreign policy;
  - 9/11 "truther" material and "New World Order" themes.
- **UFOs and the Ummo affair**, treated sympathetically. Oddly, this sits beside a careful **skeptical series debunking paranormal tricks**.
- **Personal and creative work**: songs, paintings, crafts, humour, reading notes, travel diaries, books, his non-profit association *Savoir Sans Frontières* and requests for donations.

The site's perspective is that of a credentialed outsider. Petit sees himself as a scientist marginalized by an establishment shaped by military funding and conformity, and he applies the same distrust to governments and the media. The site's defining trait is how these sit side by side: rigorous mathematics and peer-reviewed physics next to conspiracy content, skepticism next to credulity, and polemic next to tender personal material.

## 4. Highlights

1. **Real contributions to mathematical visualization.**
